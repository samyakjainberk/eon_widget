Q changing vs Q fixed

Three ways of choosing the function Hessian Q = ∇²f, everything else identical: evolving (recomputed at θt every step), fixed at init (the true ∇²f(θ₀), frozen), and random-fixed (a random symmetric Q drawn at θ₀, Frobenius-matched to the true one — same magnitude, random structure). The GD step never uses Q, so loss, Hessian sharpness, and the residual↔NTK alignment are identical for all three (they coincide). The three diverge only in the quantities that actually use Q — the Mr = ΣrkQk eigenvalues and the residual↔Mr-eigenvector alignment: fixed-Q overestimates the curvature as the net leaves θ₀, while random-Q (wrong structure) barely aligns with the residual. The bottom row ports the widget's §6 phases-of-learning diagnostics — the Qr→JTr alignment (phase 1), the power iteration (phase 2·1, Q-independent), and the residual dominance ‖J̇·r‖−‖J·ṙ‖ (phase 2·2), whose sign flip marks progressive-sharpening onset — each three-way where it depends on Q.
